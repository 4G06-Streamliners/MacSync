\section{Problem Statement}

\subsection{Problem}
The McMaster Engineering Society (MES) organizes large-scale events such as Fireball Formal, Graduation Formal,
 and Pub Nights, often drawing several hundred attendees. Currently, registration, ticketing, waiver collection,
 and check-in processes are fragmented across multiple platforms (Google Forms, spreadsheets, Discord, Instagram,
 etc.). This lack of centralization increases the administrative burden on student organizers and creates a
 confusing, inconsistent experience for attendees.\\

% TA Feedback (Problem Statement): Added explicit statement of the team's main objective/focus and how it fits within a larger integrated project, addressing "what" the team will deliver and reducing ambiguity.
\noindent The main objective of this capstone subproject is to deliver a centralized event management platform for MES
that supports both (1) attendee-facing workflows (discovering events, registering, paying, receiving
tickets/QR codes, and checking in) and (2) organizer/admin workflows (event setup, capacity management,
waiver handling, communication, and reporting). Because our work is part of a larger integrated initiative, 
our focus is on the end-to-end event registration/ticketing and operational administration slice that
can integrate cleanly with other groups' components via documented APIs and shared data conventions.
\\
\\Students struggle to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Access clear, consolidated event information.
    \item Register efficiently for events and related logistics (tables, buses).
    \item Receive timely updates or reminders.
\end{itemize}
Organizers struggle to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Manage ticket sales, waitlists, and attendee data in one place.
    \item Track accessibility/dietary requirements effectively.
    \item Minimize repetitive manual effort across multiple tools.
\end{itemize}
The absence of a centralized system reduces event memorability, increases missed opportunities, and 
wastes volunteer time that could be spent on event quality rather than administration.

% TA Feedback (Problem Statement): Explicitly surfaced project scope questions (security/privacy, platform targets, integrations, performance expectations) so the reader understands key constraints and concerns at the "what" level.
This project must also account for \textbf{security and privacy} (personal details, waiver acknowledgements, and payment-related records), \textbf{platform scope} (a mobile-first attendee experience plus a web-based admin interface), potential \textbf{third-party integrations} (payment processors and notification services), and \textbf{performance expectations} for high-traffic registration windows (e.g., several hundred users attempting to purchase/register within minutes of ticket release).

\subsection{Inputs and Outputs}

\paragraph{Inputs:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Student event registrations (tickets, RSVPs, bus signups, table preferences).
    \item Waiver acknowledgements and personal details (e.g., dietary and accessibility requirements).
    \item Payment details for ticket purchases.
    \item Admin inputs for event setup (ticket types, capacities, schedules, notifications).
\end{itemize}


\paragraph{Outputs:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Confirmation of event registration and digital tickets/QR codes.
    \item Notifications and reminders about events.
    \item Waitlist updates and allocations.
    \item Admin dashboards showing ticket sales, attendee demographics, accessibility/dietary data, and financial tracking.
    \item Check-in validation at event entry points.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Stakeholders}
% TA Feedback (Stakeholders): Replaced strict primary/secondary/tertiary hierarchy with a clearer, non-hierarchical stakeholder view to avoid unnecessary granularity and better reflect overlapping influence/interest.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MES Event Attendees (students)}: Discover events; register/RSVP; purchase tickets; manage bus/table sign-ups; receive updates; present QR code for check-in.
    \item \textbf{MES Event Organizers/Volunteers}: Create/manage events; configure capacities and ticket types; handle waivers; manage waitlists; communicate updates; run check-in operations.
    \item \textbf{MES Executives}: Require financial summaries, auditability, and risk-management visibility (e.g., waiver completion rates, incident reporting readiness).
    \item \textbf{Sponsors/Partners}: Interested in consistent event execution and any agreed sponsor visibility/engagement mechanisms.
    \item \textbf{McMaster University Administration (indirect)}: Concerned with compliance, liability posture (waivers), and student experience outcomes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Environment}
\paragraph{Hardware Environment:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Attendees: Smartphones (iOS/Android) and laptops for registration, notifications, and check-in.
    \item Organizers: Laptops/desktops for backend dashboards, mobile devices for on-site management and QR code scanning.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Software Environment:}
\begin{itemize}

    \item Web-based admin dashboard for event creation, operations, and analytics.
    \item Cross-platform mobile app for students (primary attendee interface), with fallback web access for basic attendee actions if needed.
    \item Payment integration (e.g., Stripe, Square, Paypal).
    \item Backend database to store user, event, and financial data.
\end{itemize}


\section{Goals}


\paragraph{Centralized Registration \& Ticketing}
\begin{itemize}
% TA Feedback (Goal Content): Rewrote goals to include measurable success criteria (quantitative/qualitative) in addition to description and rationale.
    \item \textbf{Intent:} Consolidate event discovery, registration/RSVP, and ticket purchase into one platform for attendees and organizers.
    \item \textbf{Measurable success criteria:} For a pilot event, at least \textbf{90\%} of registrations and ticket purchases occur through the platform (not external forms/spreadsheets), and at least \textbf{80\%} of surveyed attendees report the registration process as ``clear'' or ``very clear''.
    \item \textbf{Rationale:} Reduces confusion caused by scattered tools and ensures students have a single source of truth for event participation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Payment Integration}
\begin{itemize}
% TA Feedback (Goal Content): Added measurable success criteria and clarified outcome-focused wording.
    \item \textbf{Intent:} Provide secure online payments for ticketed events through a supported payment processor.
    \item \textbf{Measurable success criteria:} For ticketed events, \textbf{100\%} of successful purchases generate a confirmation and ticket/QR code within \textbf{60 seconds}; payment failures produce a clear error and do not create duplicate tickets.
    \item \textbf{Rationale:} Enables fast payments using established payment systems while reducing cash-handling risk and manual reconciliation for organizers.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Role-Based Access Control (RBAC/FBAC)}
\begin{itemize}
% TA Feedback (Goal Content): Added measurable criteria and tied goal to organizer workflow outcomes.
    \item \textbf{Intent:} Implement granular organizer permissions so only authorized roles can perform sensitive actions (e.g., refunds, capacity overrides, exports).
    \item \textbf{Measurable success criteria:} All organizer endpoints/actions enforce authorization checks; in acceptance testing, a user without the required role is \textbf{unable} to access restricted actions, and audit logs record \textbf{who} performed sensitive operations.
    \item \textbf{Rationale:} Reduces operational errors, supports accountability, and protects attendee data and financial actions.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Bus \& Table Sign-ups}
\begin{itemize}
% TA Feedback (Goal Content): Made the goal measurable and explicitly tied to overbooking prevention.
    \item \textbf{Intent:} Support reservations for limited resources (buses, tables) with automatic capacity tracking.
    \item \textbf{Measurable success criteria:} The system prevents overbooking (capacity is never exceeded) and updates availability in near real time; for pilot events, organizers can reconcile allocations without manual spreadsheets.
    \item \textbf{Rationale:} Simplifies logistics and reduces manual coordination during high-volume registration periods.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Notifications \& Reminders}
\begin{itemize}
% TA Feedback (Goal Content): Added measurable criteria and kept goal framed as an outcome rather than an implementation detail.
    \item \textbf{Intent:} Provide timely attendee communications (confirmations, updates, reminders, cancellations).
    \item \textbf{Measurable success criteria:} Confirmation messages are delivered immediately after registration/purchase; organizers can send an update to all attendees within \textbf{5 minutes} of composing it, and attendees report receiving critical changes (time/location) before the event in post-event feedback.
    \item \textbf{Rationale:} Improves event memorability, reduces missed opportunities, and lowers no-show risk.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Analytics \& Reporting}
\begin{itemize}
% TA Feedback (Goal Content): Added measurable reporting targets aligned with organizer/executive needs.
    \item \textbf{Intent:} Provide organizers/executives with reporting on ticket sales, attendance/check-in rates, and waitlists.
    \item \textbf{Measurable success criteria:} Dashboards update with new registrations/purchases within \textbf{1 minute}; after an event, organizers can export a summary (sales + attendance) without manual spreadsheet merging.
    \item \textbf{Rationale:} Enables decision-making during planning and provides objective post-event evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Attendee Experience}
\begin{itemize}
% TA Feedback (Goal Content): Made usability/accessibility expectations measurable via user feedback and observable criteria.
    \item \textbf{Intent:} Deliver a mobile-first, intuitive, and accessible attendee experience for registration and check-in.
    \item \textbf{Measurable success criteria:} In usability testing, at least \textbf{80\%} of participants can complete registration and locate their ticket/QR code without assistance; accessibility considerations (e.g., readable contrast and clear error messaging) are verified through a defined checklist during testing.
    \item \textbf{Rationale:} Reduces barriers to use, supports inclusivity, and increases engagement for a broad student audience.
\end{itemize}

% TA Feedback (Number of goals): Added an explicit security/privacy goal, as suggested, to reflect handling of personal and payment-adjacent data.
\paragraph{Security \& Privacy}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Intent:} Protect attendee personal information and reduce security/privacy risk in storage, access, and operational use.
    \item \textbf{Measurable success criteria:} Sensitive attendee attributes are access-controlled; organizer access is logged for sensitive actions; no payment card data is stored by the system; and privacy expectations are documented (what data is collected and why) in user-facing text.
    \item \textbf{Rationale:} The platform handles identifiable information and waiver-related records, requiring careful protection to maintain trust and reduce organizational risk.
\end{itemize}

\section{Stretch Goals}

\end{itemize}

\section*{Appendix --- Reflection}


\input{../Reflection.tex}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item What went well while writing this deliverable?
    \\
    \\The team was able to understand the problem quickly because of each member's familiarity with MES events and how they are currently run.
     From our own experiences, we already understood the main pain points as well as the features that would be most valuable
     to both organizers and attendees. This made it easier to distinguish between core goals of the project and additional features. Since we
     had a clear outline of what the system should do provided by the supervisor, our team was able to divide tasks quickly and effectively
      for this deliverable. Collaboration went smoothly, and we tackled the work for this deliverable in a fast and organized way. Having shared context
      gave us confidence in identifying the users' needs and building a well-structured document. Being able to use github issues to organize and divide
      the work also helped us stay on track and ensure that everyone contributed equally.
    \item What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and how
    did you resolve them?
    \\
    \\One of the biggest challenges was that we did not know which project we would be assigned until very close to the deadline.
     Especially, since this project is not a traditional capstone project, in the way that it is divided amongst two other groups, and the scope is quite large.
     This made it difficult to start early and limited the amount of feedback we could receive from the TA before submission. Even
      though we already understood the problem well, it was sometimes difficult to frame our knowledge at a high-level overview
     instead of diving into details. Another challenge was technical: setting up LaTeX and repositories was new for some of us and
      was time-consuming to get familiar with. We also struggled to clearly differentiate between goals vs. stretch goals, and 
      primary vs. secondary stakeholders (especially since both students and organizers are highly active users). Finally, balancing
     the level of detail such as being specific enough to show thought in requirements, but high-level enough to avoid over-scoping was an ongoing
      challenge. We resolved these issues through group discussions, reviewing example documents, and iterating on the structure.

    \item How did you and your team adjust the scope of your goals to ensure
    they are suitable for a Capstone project (not overly ambitious but also of
    appropriate complexity for a senior design project)?
    \\
% TA Feedback (Reflection): Kept Q3 as a team-wide response (as requested) but tightened wording to emphasize scope control and subproject boundaries.
    \\
    \\As a team, we adjusted scope by prioritizing a clear, end-to-end ``core path'' for MES events: event discovery, registration/ticketing, payment (when applicable), waiver acknowledgement, and check-in. We treated additional features (recommendations, sponsor tooling, post-event engagement) as stretch goals rather than core goals. Since the overall initiative is integrated across multiple groups, we also clarified our subproject boundary to reduce dependencies: we aim to deliver a complete attendee and organizer workflow slice that can integrate with other components through well-defined interfaces, without requiring other groups to finish first. This kept our goals ambitious but feasible within the capstone timeline.
\end{enumerate}
